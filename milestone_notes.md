# Milestone 1 — Community Observation

## Community

r/nba

I chose r/nba because it is a large public basketball community with a wide variety of discussion. I read around 40 posts and comments before deciding on any labels.

## Candidate distinctions I observed

1. **Evidence-backed reasoning vs. unsupported claims**

   Some posts support an opinion with statistics, player performance, game events, matchups, injuries, or other specific basketball information. Other posts make a strong claim without explaining why.

2. **Detailed discussion vs. immediate reaction**

   Some posts explain an idea or opinion in multiple sentences and give reasoning. Others are short reactions expressing excitement, frustration, surprise, humor, or disappointment.

3. **Analysis vs. prediction**

   Some posts discuss why something happened in a completed game or how a player or team performed. Others focus on what they think will happen in the future, such as playoff results, awards, trades, or player performance.

4. **Specific/checkable information vs. subjective opinion**

   Some posts include statistics, scores, records, dates, or specific game events that could be checked. Others mainly express personal judgment or preference.




   # Milestone 2 — Label Taxonomy

## 1. report

**Definition:** A post that mainly reports an NBA event, transaction, injury, quote, result, or highlight without developing the poster's own argument.

**Example 1:**  
"[Charania] Just in: Sacramento Kings guard Ben Simmons is believed to have sustained a torn Achilles tendon at practice Sunday..."

**Example 2:**  
"[Charania] Phoenix Suns center Oso Ighodaro has agreed to a three-year, $29 million contract extension through the 2030-31 season..."

## 2. analysis

**Definition:** A post that makes a basketball claim or judgment and supports it with specific facts, statistics, comparisons, game information, or developed reasoning.

**Example 1:**  
"[Quinn] Bronny hasn't reached the point of offensive viability in 2 years and the defensive threshold is so damn high at his size..."

**Example 2:**  
"[OC] People debate over the bar for making the Hall of Fame for players, but nobody does for coaches. I've analyzed all 22 coaches in the HoF..."

## 3. take

**Definition:** A post that mainly expresses a judgment, prediction, joke, emotional response, or strong basketball opinion without a developed evidence-backed argument.

**Example 1:**  
"With Ben Simmons now out with a torn Achilles injury... Horrible to see, especially as the season hasn't even started. Seems like another season with possibly even more players having the same injury."

**Example 2:**  
"Cavs struggle to make a half court shot and Kenny Atkinson has to take matters in his own hands."

## Hardest Boundary — analysis vs. take

If a post supports its conclusion with a specific statistic, comparison, game event, or concrete basketball reason, label it `analysis`.

If the post mainly gives a judgment, prediction, joke, or emotional reaction without developed supporting reasoning, label it `take`.

## Additional Rule — report vs. analysis

If a post mainly repeats or summarizes news, a quote, transaction, injury, result, or highlight without adding its own argument, label it `report`, even if the report contains statistics.

## AI Stress Test

I tested the taxonomy on eight borderline examples.

1. Factual trade announcement → `report`
2. Unsupported judgment about a team's bench → `take`
3. Same judgment supported by bench-scoring statistics → `analysis`
4. Short humorous reaction to a trade → `take`
5. Player stat line only → `report`
6. Player performance explained using matchup and shooting information → `analysis`
7. Unsupported All-Star prediction → `take`
8. Injury report from a reporter → `report`

The examples could be classified consistently using the written decision rules, so I kept the three-label taxonomy.
